# PDF Diva: BrandScript (English)

The core message of PDF Diva, following the StoryBrand framework (SB7, Donald Miller). The customer is the hero; PDF Diva is the guide. The PDF is the star of the show: the technician makes it shine from the booth. Every promotional text, the website and the Store listing should follow this story.

## One-liner

> When a speaker turns up with a PDF, most viewers give you no presenter view. PDF Diva turns any PDF into a proper presentation in one click, so the talk runs as smoothly as a PowerPoint.

Shorter variants:

- "A speaker shows up with a PDF? Press Present. PDF Diva handles the rest."
- "The presenter view PDFs never had."

## 1. The character (the hero)

The person who runs the room: venue and AV technicians, streaming and video operators, stage managers.

**What they want:** every talk to run smoothly, whatever file the speaker brings.

## 2. The problem

- **Villain:** the last-minute PDF. "My slides are a PDF" five minutes before the talk.
- **External:** PDF viewers can go full screen but have no presenter view. The speaker either presents blind or you mirror the screens, and the audience sees the viewer's toolbars.
- **Internal:** improvising under pressure, in front of an audience, with the speaker looking at you. Not knowing what will happen with someone else's file.
- **Philosophical:** presenting a PDF shouldn't be harder than presenting a PowerPoint.

## 3. The guide (PDF Diva)

- **Empathy:** "We know what it's like to get a USB stick a minute before the talk."
- **Authority:**
  - Built for the booth: tested with three displays, with cables unplugged and plugged back in mid-show.
  - Uses the same keys as PowerPoint, so any presentation remote works.
  - Built-in PDF renderer: it doesn't depend on what's installed on the machine.
  - Free and open source, in the Microsoft Store, for Windows, Mac and Linux.

## 4. The plan

**Process plan:**

1. Install PDF Diva once, on the machines in your room.
2. Open the speaker's PDF.
3. Press Present.

**Agreement plan** (what removes the fear of trying it): free, no account, no ads, no telemetry, works offline, and it never changes your default PDF viewer.

## 5. Call to action

- **Direct:** Download PDF Diva (Microsoft Store on Windows; installer, Mac and Linux on GitHub).
- **Transitional:** Watch the 30-second video. Keep the press kit for your team.

## 6. Failure (what's at stake)

- A speaker presenting blind, turning around to read their own slides.
- The desktop, the file browser or the viewer's toolbars on the projector.
- Fumbling with display settings while the room waits.
- A cable comes loose and the presentation is gone.

## 7. Success

- The PDF runs like a PowerPoint: slide on the projector, presenter view for the speaker.
- Any remote works; black screen on cue.
- If a cable comes loose, the show goes on.
- The technician is calm and the speaker looks good.

**Transformation (identity):** from the person who improvises with whatever the speaker brings to the person who has it covered.

## Using it

- Talk about the technician and their problem first, then about PDF Diva.
- Every text answers three questions fast: what is it, how does it make my life better, how do I get it.
- Website header: what it offers (a presenter view for any PDF), how it helps (the talk runs like a PowerPoint), how to get it (download button, visible without scrolling).
- Keep the tone of the product: the message is clear and calm, not hype. The name carries the character: the PDF is the star. Let it show in small nods at the edges (the closing line, the end card, the example slides), one or two per piece, never in the headline or in the problem.
